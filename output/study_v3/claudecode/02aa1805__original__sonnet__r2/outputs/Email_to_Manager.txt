Ranking is my judgment: all seven pass the screen equally, so I ordered them by reported pumpage as a rough proxy for proven yield, with the zero-pumpage Farmer City wells below the pumping ones. Farmer City is also the only system with several qualifying wells, which gives redundancy. Weldon and Waynesville are small systems (about 450 and 510 people served) and look like second-tier options.

Why the others dropped out
- Springerton, Enfield, Crossville, Bartlett: no well passes. Springerton, Enfield and Crossville wells are all abandoned and 60-410 ft deep. Bartlett wells are sealed, emergency or disconnected, or are the wrong depth or aquifer (Bartlett Well 6 is Sand & Gravel but is an emergency well at 67 ft).
- Norris City: both wells are active Sand & Gravel but only 97 and 124 ft deep.
- Near misses: Waynesville WL40005 (Well 8, the primary well) is 207 ft, 7 ft over the limit. Weldon WL01791 (Well 6) is 350 ft. Weldon WL45049 is marked INACTIVE.
- Farmer City WL47643 and WL47645 are in range but marked ABANDONED, and WL47644 is 90 ft.

Caveats before we act on this
- These are municipal public-supply wells serving the towns. The factsheet data does not tell us whether any of this water is available for industrial use. Next step would be to approach the owner and, as I understand it, check Illinois EPA and local permitting. I have not researched the permitting.
- The factsheet gives no yield or capacity, and the pumpage units are not stated. Pumpage is only a rough indicator. We should get pumping capacity, water quality and any use restrictions from the owner and Illinois EPA before shortlisting further.
- The screen follows the criteria on the well description text, as specified. The Status column also shows "A" for all seven.
- Our required water volume was not part of this exercise, so I have not tested whether any of these wells can supply it.
