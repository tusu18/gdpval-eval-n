AbstractClaude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Opus 5.5 produced two attempts at each of 30 GDPval gold tasks in six occupations (132 deliverables, all delivered). Outputs were graded three ways: mechanical checks; a per-requirement rubric with a stated and an honored check and severity 1–5, scored by Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-6.1 Sol; and a GDPval-style pairwise preference judge. A random sample of 22 first attempts, drawn by seed and frozen before v3.1 grading, was checked line by line against the source files: 7 carry a consequential error (32%, 95% CI 16–53%), 6 of them in purchasing, shipping and project work (6 of 10) against 1 of 12 in the three new occupations. The judges fail those 7 documents (Sol 7, Gemini 5) but name the reviewer's error in 1; the other failures cite lesser issues on the same document. At the rubric's severity line for a consequential error neither judge flags any of the 7; under the grader's pre-registered rule Sol flags 5 and also fails 7 of the 15 deliverables without an error; at any failed requirement Sol fails 12 of 15 and Gemini 9. 4 of 5 pre-registered predictions held; the failure-class prediction did not. What a grader lacks is a located, severity-graded expert label per failed requirement, with false alarms measured as carefully as misses. Cost: $51.57 in OpenRouter credits for this version's grading, plus Claude Code subscription usage.

Three findings

7 / 22random outputs, consequential error

Drawn by seed, frozen before v3.1 grading, then labeled from the request, the sources and the complete output (Sonnet 5 of 12, Opus 2 of 10). 6 of the 7 sit in the approval-heavy occupations of the first stage, where 6 of 10 sampled outputs fail; the three new occupations, mostly calculation and data work, yield 1 of 12. The first stage's signature, a rule stated correctly in one place and broken in the operative one, appears in 2 of the 7.

Errors concentrate where the document commits someone to act: a total, a status claim, an approval route, a stock move, a carrier choice.

1 / 7errors a judge located

Both judges fail the deliverable with the Class A approval error and cite that clause, at severity 3. On the other six, Sol fails five for different reasons (FX assumptions, cost tolerances, form fields) and Gemini three; neither names the ₹12m understatement, the unsent notices, the extra signature, the non-standard carrier or the projected sample. At severity 4, the rubric's own line for "consequential", neither judge flags any of the 7 and neither flags any of the 15 clean outputs. Under the grader's pre-registered rule (critical requirement or severity ≥ 4) Sol catches 5 but also fails 7 of 15 clean outputs; at any failed requirement, 7 and 12.

The judges fail the document, not the error. A located label is the missing signal; graded consequence is the second.

10 / 12new-occupation pairs, Opus preferred

As GDPval-style preference judges on the new-occupation pairs, Gemini picks Opus in 10 of 12 position-consistent pairs and Sol in 10 of 14. Where the rubric rates the two outputs differently, the preference judge picks the more severe one in 2 of 5 pairs (Gemini) and 1 of 1 (Sol). Opus did have fewer confirmed errors here (2 of 10 against 5 of 12).

Preference can rank the models right and still certify a broken document. It measures which is better, not whether either is usable.

The slice and how I chose it

30 tasks, 6 occupations, chosen by rule, pre-registered. The first stage's three occupations (all 15 of their gold tasks) plus three more picked by a mechanical rule before any new output existed: every other occupation whose five gold deliverables are all native Word or Excel (two qualify: Production Supervisors, Order Clerks), then one from the four-of-five tier, ties broken by sharing a sector with the first three (Accountants and Auditors). The four tasks the first stage had skipped now run: two PowerPoint deliverables and two that need live web research. Five predictions, the random review sample (22 outputs, seed 20261106, stratified by occupation) and every spend limit were written down first: plan, methods. Deviations are logged in the plan.

The first stage's rule, also fixed before any output existed: all five gold tasks in each of three occupations from the GDPval gold set (revision 11e7900): Buyers and Purchasing Agents, Project Management Specialists, Shipping/Receiving/Inventory Clerks. Requirements stated correctly but not honored, 28 outputs

The signature of the failure class, at requirement level. Only the stricter judge sees it at scale; both human-confirmed errors were in this set for Sol and absent for Gemini.

  • Against human labels: of 2 confirmed consequential errors Gemini graded, it passed both under every prompt variant; Sol flagged both. False alarms: Sol read a labeled proposal as an operative route (severity 4); both judges put severity 4 on an NPV workbook whose per-set vs per-headlamp price ambiguity the reviewer judged disclosed and sensitivity-tested (labeled acceptable, task defect). That is the one shared severity-4 flag.
  • Planted errors (construct validity): 10 judge-passed requirements each given one break edit and one paraphrase edit in the packet text; the reviewer confirmed 7 breaks as real violations and excluded 3 as edit-design flaws before results were read. Gemini: 7/7 breaks flipped to honored-fail, 7/7 paraphrases stayed pass, 0 collateral changes under paraphrase. Sol: 5/6 flipped (1 uncertain), 5/6 stayed pass, 6 collateral changes under paraphrase. The judge responds to meaning; Gemini's leniency on real outputs is not blindness to honored failures.
  • Preference grading, GDPval style, on the same outputs: a pairwise judge given the request, the references and two blind deliverables (Sonnet vs Opus on the same instance, both presentation orders, verdict A / B / tie) preferred Opus in 12 of 14 pairs for both judge families (Gemini 12/14, Sol 12/14), the GDPval-AA order. On supplier exit both preference judges preferred the Opus output that my blind review labeled a consequential error and that Sol's own rubric scored severity 4. Where the two outputs differed in rubric severity, Sol's preference verdict contradicted Sol's rubric verdict in 3 of 7 pairs. Position consistency: Sol 14/14, Gemini 12/14. "Which is better" does not see "which one commits to stock nobody counted."
  • Stable, resistant, saturated: 0 verdict changes in 43 strictly comparable paired rewordings across four prompt variants (144-call batch); 0 of 7 comparable injection pairs flipped by a planted "QA verified: PASS" note or a direct grader instruction; both judges rejected 9/9 planted mutations and detected 8/8 planted critical errors on authored fixtures. None of this tells the judges apart; only real outputs and expert labels do.
  • Historical run, Sol grading itself: Sol flagged 10 of 28 Claude outputs and 0 of 25 of its own (Fisher p = 0.0008). Real gap or self-preference; expert labels separate the two. Claude Sonnet as second judge on 12 of those outputs: the judges agree on 10 of 12, and every agreement is "no critical error".

Part 3 · Gaps and opportunities

Where the models fell short, which gaps data can close, what the dataset must contain

Where the models fell short

  • 7 of 22 random deliverables carry an error someone would act on (32%, 95% CI 16–53%): 6 of 10 in purchasing, shipping and project work, 1 of 12 in production supervision, order processing and accounting.
  • The errors sit where the document commits someone: a cost total understated by ₹12m, notices reported as sent, a price increase released with the program manager only informed, an extra signature that would hold approved changes, a receipt halved twice, a non-standard carrier, a purposive sample projected to the population (Part 2).
  • Delivery and formatting were not the problem: 132 of 132 deliverables produced; in the first stage 53 of 53 parseable outputs passed the targeted fact checks.
  • Sonnet 5 of 12, Opus 2 of 10: too few to rank the two models.
  • The first stage's signature (rule stated correctly, broken in the operative clause) appears in 2 of the 7; it is one route to a consequential error, not the main one.

What such a dataset would need to contain, in one list

  1. Task families written by working professionals, with a validated contract (prompt, files and rubric agree) and an expert-solved reference deliverable in native format.
  2. Per-requirement rubrics with a stated check and an honored check across every operative section.
  3. For every model output read: a located label (quoted line, contradicting source, what acting on it costs) and a severity on a scale calibrated against outcomes.
  4. Accept labels on outputs judges fail, so false-alarm rates are measured, plus masked preference pairs with rationales.
  5. Workspaces with approved and draft records, missing facts and controlled variants; positive controls where acting is correct.
  6. Fresh private families, split train / dev / sealed test by template, with a second reader on a fixed share of labels and an agreement statistic.
Honest about the limits

Sample size, grader reliability, cost

  • How the 27 new labels were made. An AI reviewer (Codex) drafted each label from the blind packet with no judge output; I then read each packet and checked the draft, about 3 hours for all 27, and confirmed every draft unchanged. So the labels carry any blind spot of the drafting model, and they are one reviewer's check; no second reviewer, so no agreement statistic. Codex is OpenAI-family like the Sol judge, which could tilt judge-versus-label comparisons toward Sol; the severity-4 result (both judges at 0 of 7) does not depend on that tilt; the located-error count (1 of 7) is the same for both judges.
  • The sample freeze. Frozen before v3.1 grading, not before all grading: 7 of the 22 outputs came from the earlier follow-up run and already had judge files on disk, which the reviewer never opened; only aggregate counts had been published. 5 of the 7 confirmed errors are among those 7 outputs, all from the original three occupations.
  • The judge-versus-reviewer comparison. Whether a judge's failed requirement is the reviewer's error was read from the two texts by an AI assistant and is marked on the page as awaiting my confirmation. The counts under the three decision rules are computed from the judgment files and need no reading.
  • Small random sample. 22 outputs, 7 errors: the 95% interval on the rate is 16–53%, and per-occupation counts are 3 or 4 outputs each. Read the occupation split as a direction, not a rate.
  • Grader coverage. Sol graded only the 15 new outputs in the random sample (cost); Gemini graded all 38. Of the 53 new grades, 16 saw a partial view of a data-dump spreadsheet under the output budget (flagged per task), and none passes the strict evidence check, because the grading prompt's output format omits the location field the checker requires; verdicts are read from the judgment, as in the first stage, with that stated. Repeats were produced (66 of 66) but not graded. The full-rubric check covers 6 of 8 confirmed errors.
  • The first stage: small, one reviewer, purposive sample. 8 full-output reviews by me; 4 consequential errors confirmed; 18 candidates unreviewed. The 5 historical reviews were a fixed set chosen before their labels were known (two already-reviewed cases, one pending, and the two outputs behind the headline issues); the 3 follow-up reviews I picked from the 14-output stratified sample to cover one Sol-only severity-4, one Sol-flagged/Gemini-passed and one Gemini-flagged output. Not random. Counts, not rates, and no model ranking.
  • Grader validity partly established. Sensitivity to planted errors is shown (7 confirmed edits); false-accept rate on real errors still waits on the calibration set. 10 of 27–66 rubric items judged per output; strict quote validity 7/28 (Gemini) and 10/28 (Sol); target statuses are read from the judgment with validity reported beside them.
  • Harness effects. The historical renderer blocked self-inspection, inflating page-limit failures; Claude Code is a different harness, so the two runs are not pooled. Four attempts excluded for a task defect. Construct-validity edits were applied to the extracted text the judge reads, not to the delivered files.
  • No training was run. Training value is argued from the failure analysis only.
