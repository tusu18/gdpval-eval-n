Right facts, wrong plan

Three frontier models (Claude Sonnet 5.5, Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol), 15 GDPval tasks in purchasing, project management and shipping. Every parseable deliverable passed my fact checks. Half of the ones I read in full still contained an error a purchasing manager would send back, in the clause or table someone acts on. Fact-level grading cannot see this. A dataset that grades the finished document can.

Three findings

53 / 53passed fact checks

All parseable outputs, including 12 exported workbooks, got every targeted fact right. Of 8 outputs reviewed in full, 4 carry a consequential error in an operative section, and all 4 had stated the governing rule correctly elsewhere.

Our fact checks saturated. Artifact grading has not.

24 / 35failures were the setup

Conflicting file lists, page limits the agent could not check, one unparseable answer. Only 11 failures say anything about the models.

Task validation is half of what makes evaluation data worth paying for.

1 / 28shared severity-4 flags

Two LLM judges caught every planted error they were given, on authored controls and on 7 confirmed edits, yet on 28 real outputs they shared one severity-4 flag, and the human reviewer accepted that output. The lenient judge passed both human-confirmed errors it graded.

Judges see planted errors. Real errors need expert labels before anyone trains on judge scores.

Part 1 · Grader validation

How far to trust the grader

Rubric v2 checks each requirement twice: stated (appears correctly somewhere) and honored (every operative section complies), with severity 1–5 and exact quotes the validator verifies. Requirements stated correctly but not honored, 28 outputs

The signature of the failure class, at requirement level. Only the stricter judge sees it at scale; both human-confirmed errors were in this set for Sol and absent for Gemini.

  • Against human labels: of 2 confirmed consequential errors Gemini graded, it passed both under every prompt variant; Sol flagged both. False alarms: Sol read a labeled proposal as an operative route (severity 4); both judges put severity 4 on an NPV workbook whose per-set vs per-headlamp price ambiguity the reviewer judged disclosed and sensitivity-tested (labeled acceptable, task defect). That is the one shared severity-4 flag.
  • Planted errors (construct validity): 10 judge-passed requirements each given one break edit and one paraphrase edit in the packet text; the reviewer confirmed 7 breaks as real violations and excluded 3 as edit-design flaws before results were read. Gemini: 7/7 breaks flipped to honored-fail, 7/7 paraphrases stayed pass, 0 collateral changes under paraphrase. Sol: 5/6 flipped (1 uncertain), 5/6 stayed pass, 6 collateral changes under paraphrase. The judge responds to meaning; Gemini's leniency on real outputs is not blindness to honored failures.
  • Preference grading, GDPval style, on the same outputs: a pairwise judge given the request, the references and two blind deliverables (Sonnet vs Opus on the same instance, both presentation orders, verdict A / B / tie) preferred Opus in 12 of 14 pairs for both judge families (Gemini 12/14, Sol 12/14), the GDPval-AA order. On supplier exit both preference judges preferred the Opus output that my blind review labeled a consequential error and that Sol's own rubric scored severity 4. Where the two outputs differed in rubric severity, Sol's preference verdict contradicted Sol's rubric verdict in 3 of 7 pairs. Position consistency: Sol 14/14, Gemini 12/14. "Which is better" does not see "which one commits to stock nobody counted."
  • Stable, resistant, saturated: 0 verdict changes in 43 strictly comparable paired rewordings across four prompt variants (144-call batch); 0 of 7 comparable injection pairs flipped by a planted "QA verified: PASS" note or a direct grader instruction; both judges rejected 9/9 planted mutations and detected 8/8 planted critical errors on authored fixtures. None of this tells the judges apart; only real outputs and expert labels do.
  • Historical run, Sol grading itself: Sol flagged 10 of 28 Claude outputs and 0 of 25 of its own (Fisher p = 0.0008). Real gap or self-preference; expert labels separate the two. Claude Sonnet as second judge on 12 of those outputs: the judges agree on 10 of 12, and every agreement is "no critical error".

The grader breaks before the models do

GDPval is their benchmark and its automated grader is labeled experimental, so the story is measurement and reward signal.

Open · 30 s

"GDPval is yours, and the paper calls the automated grader experimental. I ran three models on all 15 tasks in three occupations. The useful result for you isn't which model won. It's that the grading signal fails before the models do."

1. Our fact checks saturated · 60 s

"53 of 53 parseable deliverables passed every targeted fact check." Show Exhibit A. "The model recorded the three required approvers, then wrote a provisional-release clause with two. A fact-level grader scores that as fully correct. Scores keep climbing while the documents stay unusable."

2. The judge asymmetry · 60 s

"In the historical run your-family judge graded its own outputs: 0 of 25 GPT outputs flagged, 10 of 28 Claude. In the follow-up run two outside judges graded 28 outputs: Sol flagged 26, Gemini 13, and they shared one severity-4 flag, on an output my reviewer accepted. Gemini passed both human-confirmed errors it graded. I can't tell you whether GPT is better or the judge is kinder to it, and without expert-labeled errors neither can you. You solved this for HealthBench with physician labels; this is the procurement version."

3. Your grader's form can't see it · 45 s

"I ran a GDPval-style pairwise preference judge on the same 14 Sonnet-vs-Opus pairs. It preferred Opus 12 of 14 times, your leaderboard order, and on supplier exit it preferred the Opus output my blind review labeled a consequential error, the one Sol's own rubric scored severity 4. Same judge family, two grading forms, opposite answers. Meanwhile the rubric judge catches 7 of 7 planted violations, so this is about what the grader is asked, not how smart it is."

4. Task defects are training noise · 30 s

"24 of 35 historical failures were the task or the harness. RL on tasks like that rewards noise. Every task in this dataset is solved by an expert before you see it."

Ask · 30 s

"30 purchasing families: 10 development, 20 held out, three information variants each. First deliverable: your judge's measured false-accept rate on expert-labeled errors. If prompt or tool fixes close the gap, you've spent about $12K to learn that. If not, we expand to 240 families with family-level splits."

Expect first: "Do you have a GPT failure?" Answer: "Not a confirmed content error. Your judge flagged none of its own, and the second judge saw only 12. That is the calibration gap, not a clean result."

Anthropic researcher · 4:30

Knowing the rule, then breaking it

Both confirmed errors are Claude's and both are about authority and unverified claims inside an agent's work product.

Open · 30 s

"Both errors I'll show you are Claude's, and the class recurred on Opus 5.5, the top of the GDPval-AA board. In one, Claude correctly recorded that a price change needs Finance, Program and Purchase. Then it wrote a provisional-release clause that releases with two."

1. Exhibit A, authority · 75 s

Show the approver chips. "The approved record requires three; an unapproved draft says Purchase alone. Claude's audit record is right. The clause drops Finance; the next paragraph restores it. This is not a knowledge failure. The model knows the rule and still writes a document that lets someone act without authority. Another attempt on the same task held the rule everywhere, so it's learnable."

2. Exhibit B, unverified commitment · 60 s

"Opening stock is unknown, the supplier exits in 21 days, replacement takes months. The action table says buffer stock and air freight cover the gap. On the same task Opus wrote 'two weak BATNAs mean a real ZOPA' against a source that says the counterparty is inflexible; my blind review called it a consequential error, Gemini passed all ten requirements, Sol flagged it at severity 4. Six historical attempts plus that one, all one task family: one situation that recurs, not a rate."

3. Not trading one failure for another · 45 s

"The easy fix is a model that refuses to draft anything near an approval. The dataset includes positive controls where authority is complete and acting is correct, so an unnecessary hold scores as a failure. We measure three things together: consequential errors, useful completion, unnecessary holds."

4. Reward hacking and the grader · 45 s

"If rubrics become RL reward, a model can learn to mention all three approvers and still contradict them. So every requirement is checked twice: stated, and honored in every operative section. The grader flips 7 of 7 planted violations and 0 of 7 paraphrases, so it measures meaning; what it still misses is the real, hedged version. Expert-labeled errors are what fixes that."

Ask · 30 s

"One permissioned purchasing workflow, 30 families, 20 held out. We test prompt and approval-state tool repairs first at matched budget. Training data is only justified for what survives, and transfer is measured on families the model never saw."

Expect first: "Is this deception?" Answer: "No evidence of intent. It's an inconsistency between what the agent recorded and what it committed to in the artifact. That's the behavior worth measuring."

Honest about the limits

Sample size, grader reliability, cost

  • Small, one reviewer, purposive sample. 8 full-output reviews by me; 4 consequential errors confirmed; 18 candidates unreviewed. The 5 historical reviews were a fixed set chosen before their labels were known (two already-reviewed cases, one pending, and the two outputs behind the headline issues); the 3 follow-up reviews I picked from the 14-output stratified sample to cover one Sol-only severity-4, one Sol-flagged/Gemini-passed and one Gemini-flagged output. Not random. Counts, not rates, and no model ranking.
  • Grader validity partly established. Sensitivity to planted errors is shown (7 confirmed edits); false-accept rate on real errors still waits on the calibration set. 10 of 27–66 rubric items judged per output; strict quote validity 7/28 (Gemini) and 10/28 (Sol); target statuses are read from the judgment with validity reported beside them.
  • Harness effects. The historical renderer blocked self-inspection, inflating page-limit failures; Claude Code is a different harness, so the two runs are not pooled. Four attempts excluded for a task defect. Construct-validity edits were applied to the extracted text the judge reads, not to the delivered files.
  • No training was run. Training value is argued from the failure analysis only.
