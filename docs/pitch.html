The two pitches

OpenAI researcher · 4:30

The grader finds the line, not the stakes

GDPval is their benchmark and its automated grader is labeled experimental, so the story is measurement and reward signal.

Open · 30 s

"I ran Claude Sonnet and Opus on 30 GDPval gold tasks in six occupations, two attempts each. Before any grading I froze a random sample of 22 outputs and checked them against the source files. The result that matters to you is about the grader, not the models."

1. Severity is the miss · 75 s

"Seven of the 22 carry an error someone would act on. Both rubric judges, Sol and Gemini, rate every one of the seven below severity 4, the rubric's line for consequential. Drop to 'any failed requirement' and Sol catches all seven, but it also fails 12 of the 15 acceptable outputs. Gemini's misses came from the ten-requirement cut; on the full rubric it finds them and still scores them 3 of 5. The judge can see the line. It can't price it."

2. Preference ranks, it doesn't certify · 45 s

"As a GDPval-style pairwise judge, Gemini prefers Opus in 10 of 12 new pairs and Sol in 10 of 14, which matches Opus having fewer confirmed errors here, 2 of 10 against 5 of 12. But where the rubric rates the pair differently, the preference judge picks the more severe output in 3 of 6 such cases. Train on that and you reward the better-looking document, including the one that breaks the approval route."

3. Pre-registered, including what failed · 45 s

"Five predictions were written down before any output. Four held. The failure-class prediction didn't: in the three new occupations, mostly calculation work, 1 of 12 sampled outputs failed, against 6 of 10 in purchasing, shipping and project control. That says where label budget pays. The labels were AI-drafted and I checked all 27; the drafting model is OpenAI-family like Sol, which is why I lead with a result where both judges miss equally."

Ask · 30 s

"30 families in the approval-heavy occupations, 10 dev and 20 held out, three variants each, with expert severity labels. First deliverable: your grader's measured miss and false-alarm rates against them, about $12K. If severity calibration closes with prompting, you've learned that cheaply. If not, it's the case for a 240-family training set."

Expect first: "Is severity 3 vs 4 just a threshold choice?" Answer: "Move the threshold to 3 and the false alarms jump with it: Sol already fails 12 of 15 acceptable outputs at 'any failure'. No single cut separates the seven; that's why the labels have to carry consequence."

Anthropic researcher · 4:30

Claude errs where authority is at stake

Every confirmed error is a Claude output, and most are about approval routes and commitments in an agent's work product.

Open · 30 s

"In a random sample of 22 Claude deliverables on GDPval tasks, 7 carry an error someone would act on: Sonnet 5 of 12, Opus 2 of 10. Six of the seven are in purchasing, shipping and project control, where the document routes approvals or commits a supplier."

1. Exhibit, authority · 60 s

Show Exhibit A. "The approved record needs Finance, Program and Purchase. Claude records that correctly, then writes a provisional-release clause with two. In the new sample the same class recurs: a minor-change row lets a price increase release with Purchase and a delegated Finance controller while the programme manager is only informed, and a routing exception tells shipping staff to use a service the workbook itself classes as non-standard, against the task's standard-service rule. The model knows the rule and writes a document that lets someone act without it."

2. Not one pattern · 45 s

"Only 2 of the 7 state the rule correctly elsewhere. The rest are claims the record doesn't support, like a CPO report saying stop-work notices were already sent, and source misreads. The common root is committing to something the sources don't back."

3. Not trading one failure for another · 45 s

"14 of the 22 were usable as written. A model that refuses near any approval would fix the seven and break the fourteen. The dataset includes positive controls where authority is complete and acting is correct, so an unnecessary hold scores as a failure."

4. The grader as reward · 45 s

"Both judges find a failed requirement in nearly every output and rate none of the seven as consequential. Optimise against that and a model learns to look careful, not to be right. Every requirement is checked twice, stated and honored in every operative section, and the labels that matter are severity, from people who do the job."

Ask · 30 s

"One permissioned purchasing workflow, 30 families, 20 held out. Test prompt and approval-state tool repairs first at matched budget; training data is justified only for what survives, and transfer is measured on families the model never saw."

Expect first: "Is this deception?" Answer: "No evidence of intent. It's an inconsistency between what the agent recorded and what it committed to in the artifact. That's the behaviour worth measuring."

Hard questions

Each answer is what the record supports today. Two or three sentences on the call.

Isn't severity 3 vs 4 just a threshold choice?OpenAI

Move the cut to 3 and the false alarms move with it: at "any failure" Sol already fails 12 of 15 acceptable outputs. No single cut on either judge separates the 7 confirmed errors from the 15 acceptable outputs, which is why the labels have to carry consequence, not just pass/fail.

Your predictions: which failed?both

P1. I predicted the first stage's signature, a rule stated correctly in one place and broken in the operative one, would show up in the new occupations. It didn't: the one confirmed error there is an unsupported claim, and only 2 of all 7 errors fit the signature. The other four held, one of them (P4) only because both judges missed everything at severity 4.

Which failures go away with better tools or more compute, not data?both

Most of the 24 setup failures: page overflow needs a render-and-revise loop, the five output-cap hits need a larger budget. The 11 behavior candidates happened with the right facts already in the output, so retrieval and budget don't explain them. The pilot's first arm tests prompt and tool repairs at matched budget; data is only claimed for what survives.

Why only three occupations?both

It's six now. The first stage used three for depth: three information conditions, two models and two repeats per task, which separated model behaviour from task noise. This version adds three occupations chosen by a mechanical rule written down before any output, 30 tasks in all. Six still don't represent 44, so the occupation split is a direction: 6 of 10 sampled outputs failed in the approval-heavy three, 1 of 12 in the new three.

Who labeled the failures? Would another reviewer agree?both

For the 22-output random sample and the 5 Sol controls, an AI reviewer (Codex) drafted each label from the blind packet with no judge output, and I checked every packet against its draft, about 3 hours; I confirmed all 27 unchanged. The 8 first-stage reads I wrote myself. No second reviewer yet, so no kappa; and the drafting model is OpenAI-family, like Sol, which I flag wherever a judge comparison could tilt.

Is 0/25 vs 10/28 self-preference?OpenAI

It fits self-preference and it fits a real quality gap. This version tested it: I labeled the 5 historical GPT-6.1 Sol outputs from the two task families where Claude errors were confirmed, and none carries a consequential error. That's 5 outputs, so it weakens the judge-artifact reading without settling it.

What's your grader's false-accept rate?OpenAI

Measured now, on a random sample. At the rubric's severity-4 gate both judges pass all 7 confirmed errors and flag none of the 15 acceptable outputs. At 'any failed requirement' Sol misses none but fails 12 of 15 acceptable outputs; Gemini misses 2 and fails 9. Gemini's misses came from the ten-requirement cut, and on the full rubric it finds them, still at severity 3 or lower. The planted-error tests pass because a planted violation is unambiguous; real ones are hedged.

Your judges disagree. Why keep either?OpenAI

Because they agree on the part that matters and disagree on the part that's noise. Neither rates a confirmed error as consequential, and Gemini never flags an output Sol passes (0 of 12 in the new occupations), so Sol is the stricter of two uncalibrated scales. Expert severity labels are what turn either into a usable grader.

Both judges put severity 4 on a workbook your reviewer accepted. Who's right?both

The source leaves the price unit ambiguous (per set vs per headlamp). The output disclosed the ambiguity and sensitivity-tested it, so I labeled it acceptable with a task defect. Reasonable people can disagree; that is exactly the case an expert panel adjudicates, and why judge agreement alone is not a label.

You ran Sonnet and Opus; which is better?Anthropic

On the random sample, Opus had fewer confirmed errors, 2 of 10 against 5 of 12, and the preference judges agree (Opus in 10 of 12 and 10 of 14 consistent pairs). With 22 outputs the interval on either rate is wide, and the failure class shows up in both, so I'd say 'Opus erred less here', not 'Opus is safer'.

GDPval-AA ranks Opus 5.5 first with Elo 1866. Doesn't that settle quality?Anthropic

Elo measures which deliverable a judge prefers pairwise. Here the preference judges also picked Opus, and Opus did err less, but where the rubric rated a pair differently the preference judge picked the more severe output in 3 of 6 cases. Ranking and certifying a document are different questions.

Is this deception?Anthropic

No evidence of intent. It's an inconsistency between what the agent recorded and what it committed to in the artifact. That's the behavior worth measuring, and the stated/honored split is how.

How do you stop reward hacking?Anthropic

Rubrics score the recommended action and cross-section consistency, not mentions. Negatives include outputs with the right facts and a contradicting artifact. Positive controls make refusal lose, and every consequential label carries an expert rationale with quotes.

Why buy data instead of building an approval-state validator?both

Build the validator. The eval set is how you learn whether it's enough; if it removes the errors on fresh families, stop the training spend for that failure.

How do you know training transfers?both

I don't, and I'm not claiming it. The design splits families by source and template, trains on development families and measures on sealed ones.

GDPval is public. Isn't it contaminated?both

Possibly. The product is fresh private families built the same way; the public slice was the diagnostic.

Why not just use the official GDPval grader?OpenAI

It isn't runnable locally: the paper describes GPT-5-high comparing against expert deliverables, the prompt and the expert files aren't public, and the hosted path ended when OpenAI's Evals platform went read-only on 31 October 2026. I ran its form instead, a pairwise preference judge on my own outputs, on all 33 pairs. It ranks Opus first and still picks the more severe output in 3 of 6 cases where the rubric disagrees.

How is this different from APEX or the Remote Labor Index?both

Those grade final deliverables against expert rubrics. The distinctive parts here are workspace state with approved and draft records, controlled variants of one task, and the stated/honored split. I haven't run a head-to-head, so I won't claim it's better.

What did this cost, and what would the dataset cost?both

This version: $51.57 in OpenRouter credits ($31.38 rubric grading, $8.81 preference judging, $11.38 full-rubric check); solving ran on a Claude Code subscription, reported usage $98.16. Review time was about 3 hours for 27 label checks. The pilot estimate is about $391 per accepted family, $11.7K for 30, from assumed expert hours and yield; real numbers come from pilot time logs.

Why purchasing?both

Native Word and Excel deliverables, approval and dependency rules you can verify against source records, and consequences that are concrete: a release without Finance sign-off is an audit finding. The random sample backs the choice: 6 of 10 outputs in purchasing, shipping and project control carry a consequential error, against 1 of 12 in the three calculation-heavy occupations.
